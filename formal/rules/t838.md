| A closure a call hands back keeps its effects through closure literals, in three more shapes. First, a literal that captures it under another name — a copy `: d c`, an arm's `= d c` — and runs it: the literal's captures take the roots of what the closure does under any name it has (`__clo_run_attr`, as a run of `d` itself does), and a builder's placeholder run inside the body is noted into the literal's effects too, so a run of the literal replays it. Second, a literal whose body hands back a closure (`^ c`, or its tail): what running that does is recorded under the literal (`lr#`; its own parameters as `%k`, rewritten onto the call's arguments), and a call of the literal gives its binding those effects, as a function's `retcloeff` does. Third, a literal that hands back its own closure parameter (`\ ( @ v ) f → ( @ v ) { ^ f }`): the literal's own `retclo` is kept (`lq#`), and the closure argument at that index has its effects passed to the result, as for a function returning its argument. A closure that only reads stays accepted.
