| A binding that is a borrow on one path and its own value on another (`BorrowedShared ⊔ Owned`: a cursor a loop re-points at a fresh slice, a `?` / `??` arm that gives it a value of its own, a `continue` path) is `MaybeOwned`, no longer a plain borrow. So is a binding given, in one `let` / `=`, a value that may be its own and also borrows (a join with a fresh arm and a view arm, a call that hands back a fresh value on some calls and lends on others), and one a closure that runs gives a new value (`@assign`), and a binding that borrows given a field through itself (`= . cur name …`: codegen stores the value into the binding's copy, which releases it where the binding is given a new value). It stays a borrow to every other rule (what it borrows from may not go first). But given a new value it releases what it owned there, so what was borrowed from it ends. At its scope's end it is released as an owner is, ending what views it, what was stored into it, and the caller's holders given a view of it. A copy of it borrows it as well as what it borrows (`bck_borrow_owners`). Only a view binding the walk has seen given a value that may be its own joins to `MaybeOwned`: one given only views and literals owns nothing on any path. Before, these were read as plain borrows, and re-pointing a borrow frees nothing: open on main for a cursor over a local String's view, and reopened by #422's view parameters for h118 (`cursor_whole_rest_after_replace`) | §2.2, §7.6 | yes: made to reject more (a copy of a binding that may own its value, kept across its re-point or its scope's end, or given to the caller's storage) | none to the proof: A1's state is per path, and on the path where the binding owns its value `StmtT.assign` and the scope's end drop it, ending the borrows of its `.self`. nurlc's join kept only the borrow side of the two paths; `MaybeOwned` keeps both, as `MaybeMoved` does for a move on one path |
