| A copy of a value that holds views holds the same views (agora #362, #368, #377, #347). What a function makes of what its argument holds is derived from its walk, as #422 derives what it makes of the argument's storage: a parameter that holds views (an owner of them, a Slice, a struct of views) starts its walk holding a pseudo source of its own, `%p`, standing for the caller's views. A value handed back holding them answers `%k` (g_fn_rviews), and another parameter left holding them answers `h>%k` (g_fn_vkeeps; `%K.H` rows at the call). The caller asks what a copy of argument k holds, its views and not its storage (`g_sxe_held`): an owner's dependencies, or what a borrow's sources' elements view. `mem_dup` answers `:%0`, and publishes its operand. A callee with no walk answer (trusted or library code, compiled) whose result can hold views holds what its non-closure arguments hold, and so does a match payload of such a call. A copy taken by the call it is an argument of still holds what it held.
