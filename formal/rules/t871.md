| Early exits in the calculus: `break`, `continue` and a `^` before the end of a function's body are statements of A2 (`Stmt.block`, `Stmt.exit n`, `Stmt.loop` for `while T`). An `exit n` leaves `n + 1` blocks in one step (`ExitAt`, `Step.blockExit`), ending every scope it leaves as that scope's own end does, its drops and destructors run; typing carries the exits a statement may take (`StmtT`'s `Exits` index: `Exits.under`, `Exits.underD`, `Exits.blk`), and a block's end says no more than the context each exit leaves from. Before, the calculus had none of them: a loop was left only from its head and a function returned at its body's end, so nurlc's rules for these exits (`bck_loop`'s join of the `break`s' states, the `while T` exit of agora #453) were not rules of the calculus (agora #871) | §1, §2.6, §2.8, §7.6 | yes: extended, no statement weakened. `StmtT` gains the exit index (`.none` at a function's body and in every crown theorem, which keep their statements); preservation, progress (a statement about to exit is one more final form, `ExitAt`, never a top-level one: `exitAt_none`), the leak theorems, the renaming lemma (`RSetup.liftX`) and A3's thread lemmas cover the new forms; `ExitWitness` types a `break` out of `while T` that drops a live owned `Vec`, a `continue`, and an early `^` across a scope with its caller, each run safe and leak-free | a gain: the proof's claim now covers programs that leave a loop or a function early. The frames that are not scopes (`recover`, a borrow's, a freeze's, a lock's, an element's) take no exits (`Exits.none`); in source each holds a call, so no exit crosses one (`README.md`) |
