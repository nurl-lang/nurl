| The closures a scope releases are listed by each binding's owner slot (`<name>__envown`), not by its name: a closure bound in an inner scope, or bound again in the same one, under the name of another closure is an entry of its own, drained through its own slot (the slot's storage and type are kept for the function, so a drain needs no name). By name the two were one entry and the inner closure's environment was released by nobody (agora #614). A `:` binding's value block whose tail is a closure binding the block made takes the environment over from that binding, as `: g f` does, and a `:` settles the literal-env channel when it has bound it, so the `:` around a value block does not take an inner literal's environment for its own right-hand side's (agora #627). A closure's environment moves to a second binding (`: g f`, `= g f`) only where that binding lives at least as long as the first and no `;` defer armed before may still call the first; otherwise the second is another name of a closure the first still owns, and `= a a` releases nothing. An assignment, like a `:`, settles the literal-env channel when it has bound it. A slot binding assigned a closure that is not its own (a field's, a parameter's) stays in the list — its slot, cleared by that assignment, says on each path what is left to release — and is marked as no owner to move from until an assignment in the same or an enclosing scope gives it its own again (`mem_clo_mark_lent`): `= g f` from it makes `g` a second name the borrow walk follows to the lender, as before | §7.5 | no: releases only.
