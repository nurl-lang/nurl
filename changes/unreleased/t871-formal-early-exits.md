### Added

- **The formal proof covers `break`, `continue` and an early `^`** (Agora #871). A2's calculus has blocks, an `exit n` that leaves `n + 1` of them in one step, and `while T` as a loop left only by its exits. Every scope an exit leaves ends on the way, with its drops and destructors run as at the scope's own end. Typing carries the exits a statement may take, and a block's end says no more than the state each exit leaves from. Preservation, progress, the no-leak theorems, the renaming lemma and A3's thread proofs all cover the new forms, and every crown theorem keeps its statement. Before this, the calculus had none of these exits: a loop was left only from its head, and a function returned only at its body's end. A new module, `ExitWitness`, has three worked examples, each run memory-safe and leak-free: a `break` out of `while T` that drops a live owned `Vec`, a `continue`, and an early `^` across a scope with its caller. An exit out of a `recover`, a borrow, a freeze, a lock or an element frame is not yet in the calculus (`formal/README.md`).
