### Added

- **The memory-model proof covers taking an element that holds views out through a borrow** (AGORA-897). An example is a slice popped through `vec_at vv 0` of a `Vec` of `Vec`s of slices and kept: `: [s y ( vec_pop b )`. The new rule `StmtT.takeBV` types AGORA-889's move-out step for element types that hold views, as a truncation through the borrow that also ends the views among the owners' elements. The new binding holds what the borrow holds, and the proof shows that the element's views stay valid through it. The soundness and leak-freedom theorems cover it, with no axiom and no `sorry`. A witness program (`formal/A2/TakeBVWitness.lean`) moves two views of a String's buffer out through a borrow, keeps and reads both, and is proven safe and leak-free.
- **... also when the element's type holds a closure that holds views** (AGORA-898). The rule no longer asks that the element type hold no closure. A move-out keeps only the node's old elements, so its owner captures by name only what it did before, and the new binding holds the closure's captures through the borrow. A second witness moves a closure whose environment is a view out through `vec_at vv 0` and is proven safe and leak-free.
- **... and when the element holds a destructor value** (AGORA-899). The new binding must hand the value to its `letd` first thing and is assigned nothing else, as a call's owned result does. The `letd` then runs the destructor. A new theorem (`A2.Dtor.takeB_typed`) states this guarantee. A witness pops a destructor value through `vec_at vv 0` of a `Vec` of `Vec`s of them into its `letd`, and is proven safe and leak-free.
