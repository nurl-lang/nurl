### Added

- **The kernel builds the certificate's term for programs with destructor frames too** (AGORA-1029 step 8). `C.Elab.elabIR` (`formal/C/Elab.lean`) now also covers a struct with a `% Drop` of the program's own (A2's `.dtor f u`): its values bound by `.letd`, whose scope's end runs the destructor, a destructor struct of one field padded with a number, raw text in a field a view, the destructor's own reads of its fields through the pairs, and a destructor value moved into another binding or lent to a read-only parameter.
  - All 4 destructor programs of the 62 certify programs are now in the subset (55 in all, 51 before). The 2 the checker accepts get `Elab.lean` and `Cert.elab` (32 bundles, 30 before), so their bundles no longer trust the translator `packages/nurl-cert/src/tolean.nu`. Outside stay the channel and thread programs, and nothing else.
  - `elabIR` stays stricter than the translator, never laxer. It makes the translator's checks on destructor types (a destructor value a call hands back, a destructor binding assigned, a destructor value handed back, a destructor parameter `sink` or `inout` by its flags or the walk's summary, a destructor type with no fields or no `ty` line, a `d` drop flag on a type with no destructor of the program's). It also refuses what the translator takes: a `drop__X` whose `%X` is no struct's name (`drop__i64` makes the translator take every `i64` for a destructor value), a destructor type inside another type, a closure in a destructor struct's field, a destructor value bound from a call through a closure, a closure type taking or handing back a destructor type, and a closure's code with a variable of a destructor type. Each check has a kernel witness for its refusal and for its well-formed neighbour, and `elab_dtor` builds `destructor_frame_left_by_an_early_return`'s term.
  - `tools/ncore/elab_parity.sh` finds 0 differences and 0 programs `elabIR` takes but the translator refuses, over 243 programs in the subset (224 before). 10 new fixtures in `tools/ncore/elab/` (`dtor_*`) cover the new rules.
  - `nurl-cert` 0.4.0: the skip before Lean (`ELAB_CTORS`) takes `.letd` and `.dtor`. The selftest's program outside the subset, and its tamper bundle, is a channel program now (`chan_int_sent_and_received`).
  - Certify takes 77.2 s over the 62 certify programs against 76.7 s before (+0.7 %, best of 3 alternated runs against step 7). The 4 destructor programs new to the subset take +0.20 to +0.45 s each for their Elab.lean part (2 of them the checker refuses, and spend it too); the other 58 are 0.8 s faster in all. A word's bytes are now read lazily (`wordGo` puts each byte at the head of a cons whose tail is not yet read), so a prefix test reads only the bytes it needs. The largest peak memory is the same (2588 MiB against 2587 MiB); a new program's peak is +23 to +47 MiB, and the sum of the peaks is +0.03 % (−0.3 % over the other 58).
