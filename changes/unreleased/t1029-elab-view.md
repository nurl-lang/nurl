### Added

- **The kernel builds the certificate's term for programs with views too** (AGORA-1029 step 5). `C.Elab.elabIR` (`formal/C/Elab.lean`) now also covers slices of `Vec`s of numbers, raw text viewed by `string_data`, and `Vec`s of raw strings.
  - It lowers the library calls `string_data` and `slice_from_vec` (a view), `slice_len`, `nurl_println`, and the changes of a `Vec` through a borrow of it: `vec_clear`, `vec_truncate`, `vec_reserve`, `vec_shrink_to_fit` and `string_push_str`. It covers `foreach` over a `Vec` of numbers too: a view of the `Vec` held for the loop, each element lent through it.
  - It finds the raw text that holds a view as the translator does (`__view_vars`), and a `drop`'s check follows a view copied into a temporary (`__views_of`).
  - 32 of the 62 certify programs are now in the subset (19 before). The 18 the checker accepts get `Elab.lean` and `Cert.elab` (13 before), so their bundles no longer trust the translator `packages/nurl-cert/src/tolean.nu`.
  - `elabIR` stays stricter than the translator, never laxer. It refuses a `let x` of a library call the translator lowers by dropping `x` (`vec_reserve`, `nurl_println`, …), a `slice_from_vec` whose summary says it lends its result, a slice handed back, and a change in place of a `foreach`'s element. Each check has a kernel witness for its refusal and for its well-formed neighbour.
  - `tools/ncore/elab_parity.sh` finds 0 differences and 0 programs `elabIR` takes but the translator refuses, over 101 programs in the subset. 14 new fixtures in `tools/ncore/elab/` cover the new constructs.
  - `nurl-cert` 0.3.0: the skip before Lean (`ELAB_CTORS`) takes the new constructors (`.edit .view .sat .slen .trunc .slice`).
- **`nurl-cert words` leaves out the standard library's functions whole**, not only their statement lines, whenever Lean's reader would accept their `fn` and `v` lines. When it would not, those lines are printed, and the reader refuses the words as it refuses the whole core IR. `C.Elab.elabIR` reads nothing else of a library function, so it answers the same on the words as on every line: `tools/ncore/elab_parity.sh` checks this over the corpus, and the selftest over 11 core IRs, 8 of which the reader refuses. A program that imports `slice.nu` and `string.nu` has about a quarter of the words (`vec_cleared_then_pushed`: 407 entries to 119).
- **Cost:** over the 62 certify programs certify takes 81.3 s against 80.8 s before (+0.6 %). The 13 programs new to the subset take +0.3 to +0.8 s each for their Elab.lean part, 8 of which the checker refuses. The other 49 are 7.2 s faster: their words shrink too, and a `drop`'s check now reads only the lines that can add a view, once per function. The largest peak memory is unchanged (2584 MiB against 2586 MiB); a new program's peak is +46 to +105 MiB, and the sum of the peaks is +1.5 %.
